Research and reusable prompt tooling for AI in Indian agriculture — technologies, evidence, policy, markets, risks, and how to design/evaluate deployments that survive contact with smallholder reality.

## Headline findings from the research

- India has moved from pilots to **public infrastructure**: 11 crore Farmer IDs (ahead of the March 2027 target), a digital crop survey over 492 districts and 23.5 crore plots, and a ₹2,817 crore Digital Agriculture Mission.
- **Adoption is still thin**: agritech is ~2% of a $452 bn agricultural economy; AI is roughly a tenth of agritech, growing at ~44% CAGR ($0.9 bn → $5.6 bn, 2025–2030).
- **Evidence is real but narrower than vendor claims.** Digital advisory reliably changes knowledge and practice; yield effects are modest and context-dependent (+6.8% in an Odisha RCT's rain-affected plots, ~0 in an Andhra cashew trial). Crop-disease models drop from 95%+ on benchmarks to 70–80% in fields.
- **The binding constraint is the last mile**, not the model: rural internet is 49/100 vs 129/100 urban, ~39% of rural adults are functionally digitally illiterate, and 37% of rural women own a smartphone with home internet.
- **A realistic 2030 value pool is ₹16,000–37,000 crore/year** (bottom-up, assumptions stated) against the government's ₹70,000 crore ceiling — concentrated in loss avoidance and input efficiency rather than yield uplift.

## How to use it

1. Read the executive summary and §7 (economics) for the decision-relevant core.
2. Use §10's checklist before committing to any AI-agri deployment or investment.
3. Use the prompt pack to reproduce or extend the research for another state, crop or funder.

## A note on sources

Every material number is cited and dated in the brief. Market-size estimates are presented as ranges because definitions differ by up to 10x. Vendor impact figures (Cropin, Fasal, AgroStar) are labelled as self-reported and upward-biased by selection — the report explains why in §3.
